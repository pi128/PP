The five-story building had been built a hundred

neglected, then renovated time and again since the
first war, it had been purchased for good in 1951 by
an aggressive tax lawyer named Anthony Bendini. He
renovated it yet again and began filling it with
lawyers. He renamed it the Bendini Building. 
 
He pampered the building, indulged it, coddled it,
each year adding another layer of luxury to his
landmark. He fortified it, sealing doors and windows

occupants. He added elevators, electronic
surveillance, security codes, closed-circuit
television, a weight room, a steam room, locker rooms
and a partnersÕ dining room on the fifth floor with a
captivating view of the river. 
 
In twenty years he built the richest law firm in
Memphis, and, indisputably, the quietest. Secrecy was
